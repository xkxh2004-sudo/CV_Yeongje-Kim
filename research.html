Research

Research

My research examines how technology-supported learning environments can support participation, well-being, and equitable learning opportunities for teachers and learners. I am interested in how teachers use generative artificial intelligence to design student-centered lessons, how people learn and sustain participation in communities, and how learning experiences relate to well-being. My work spans school classrooms, higher education, and later-life learning, with attention to learners’ backgrounds, experiences, and access to support. Across these settings, I seek to understand how technology and the social conditions of learning can help people participate meaningfully. I use qualitative interviews, quantitative analyses, and analyses of interaction patterns to investigate these questions.

  • Investigating the effects of a four-week reflective activity program on international students’ well-being.

  • Investigating how learners perceive and characterize their use of AI during data analysis.
